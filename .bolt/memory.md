# Project memory

## Product intent & audience

- Create a world-class, editable 26-slide presentation for the “Beyond the Form” talk, using the DevFest PDF as visual and structural reference.
- Serve technical and general conference attendees with accessible explanations of governance, privacy, security, AI risks, and Nigerian context.
- Optimize for live presentation and review in Present mode, with speaker notes supporting delivery.

## Brand personality & voice

- Confident, energetic, friendly, and conference-ready.
- Explain complex technical and governance concepts clearly and practically without diluting substance.
- Use concise copy, strong chapter beats, and an optimistic, playful tone.
- Build toward a memorable closing statement or “mic-drop” moment.

## Visual design & UX direction

- Use a DevFest-inspired visual language: rounded cards, bold labels, bright color blocking, expressive chapter dividers, diagrams, and visual storytelling.
- Maintain presentation-scale hierarchy, generous separation between content groups, and editable/composable slide elements.
- Prefer diagrams, UI references, maps, and visual metaphors over dense prose.
- Use restrained photographic imagery as atmosphere or visual reference; keep core diagrams, cards, checklists, and technical layouts editable.
- Added visual references include conference audience, Lagos skyline, mobile finance, server infrastructure, AI/network imagery, developer coding, and collaborative governance imagery.
- Use imagery especially for security, AI, reality-check, framework, trust-question, Nigeria-context, and fictional customer-record moments.
- Support motion through staged reveals, fades, zooms, cascades, packet-movement cues, glitch-ready transitions, and section transitions.
- Treat GIF-like moments as presentation-native motion rather than embedded animated timelines; preserve editable fallbacks when richer media is unavailable.
- Speaker notes should document visual direction, motion, voiceover, sound cues, transitions, and required assets.
- Avoid letting optional media replace narrative clarity or block deck completion.

## Decisions, constraints & rationale

- Follow the source document’s exact 26-slide story rather than reproducing the DevFest PDF page-for-page.
- Narrative beats include the form problem; privacy versus security; AI access risks; prompt injection; a fictional data leak; Nigerian context; live agent attacks; the five-question framework; the full developer checklist; and the final closing statement.
- The final act uses the live agent attack demo, five-question test, and 10-point developer checklist. The supplemental statistics slide is excluded from the final sequence.
- Every slide has expanded speaker notes covering visual, motion, narration, audio, transition, and asset requirements.
- Online imagery is used selectively and should remain subordinate to editable content.
- No finished video is currently embedded; optional screen recordings, animations, and sound effects remain replaceable enhancements.
- Existing Vite/React authoring and validation workflow remains in use, with the deck kept editable.
- Build and validation checks have succeeded after note-formatting corrections and visual asset additions.

## Open questions & assumptions

- Optional higher-fidelity assets may still be supplied: fictional fintech-agent recording, Nigeria map, Nigerian developer/community photo, POS or banking UI references, biometric illustration, terminal recording, prompt-injection PDF mockup, AI avatar animation, glitch/confetti effects, shield animation, and closing music sting.
- Sourced imagery requires availability and usage-rights review before public distribution.
- Editable fallback visuals are acceptable when optional media is unavailable.
- Final export requirements remain unspecified: editable PowerPoint, rendered video, presentation mode, or a combination.
- Animation timing, automatic transitions, and media placement require a full presentation run-through.
- Talk duration, audience size, and presenter preferences may require pacing or copy-density adjustments.
